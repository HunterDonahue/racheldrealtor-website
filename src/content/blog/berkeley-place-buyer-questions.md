Berkeley Place gets a lot of interest, and with that comes a fairly consistent set of questions from buyers who are seriously considering it. Here's what actually comes up, and how I answer it honestly.

## "Is it too close to campus?"

This depends entirely on what you want. Proximity to TCU means walkability to a lot of restaurants, events, and campus activity, and yes, game days bring more noise and traffic than the rest of the year. If you value energy and convenience over total quiet, it's a feature. If you want maximum quiet, it's worth touring during a game week before you decide.

## "What's the deal with the older homes here?"

Berkeley Place has a genuinely historic housing stock, much of it from the 1920s-1940s. That means real character — and it also means buyers should budget for systems updates (electrical, plumbing) that a newer home wouldn't need. It's not a red flag, it's just part of buying something with real history.

## "How competitive is it, really?"

Well-priced, well-maintained homes in this neighborhood tend to move quickly when they hit the market. That doesn't mean every home gets multiple offers, but it does mean buyers who wait too long to make a decision on a good one often lose it. Knowing your numbers ahead of time matters more here than in a slower-moving area.

## "Can we walk to things?"

Yes, more than most Fort Worth neighborhoods. That's a big part of the appeal, and it's worth actually walking the routes you'd use regularly before you buy — a five-minute drive and a fifteen-minute walk can both technically be "close," and which one matters to you is personal.

## "Is it worth the premium over other 76109 streets?"

It depends what you're optimizing for. Location and walkability carry real value here, but so does lot size and quiet in some of the surrounding streets. Neither is objectively better — they're different trade-offs.
