I'm not a lender, and I'm always upfront about that — anything specific to your financing situation should come from a licensed loan officer. But I've watched enough first-time buyers navigate this process to see the same handful of mistakes happen over and over, and most of them are avoidable.

## Getting pre-qualified instead of pre-approved

These sound similar and are not the same thing. Pre-qualification is a rough estimate based on what you tell a lender. Pre-approval involves actual verification of your income, credit, and assets, and it's what sellers and their agents take seriously in a competitive situation. If you're only pre-qualified, you're not as ready as you think you are.

## Shopping for a house before shopping for a lender

Falling in love with a home before you know your real number is a recipe for disappointment. Talking to a lender first — even before you start touring — tells you what you can actually work with, and it means you're ready to move the moment the right home shows up instead of scrambling.

## Making a big purchase or credit change mid-process

New furniture on a store credit card, a new car loan, even a job change — any of these can affect your debt-to-income ratio and jeopardize financing after you're already under contract. The rule of thumb lenders give for a reason: don't make major financial moves between pre-approval and closing.

## Not asking what the pre-approval actually assumes

A pre-approval letter is based on the loan program, rate environment, and property type at the time it was issued. If any of those change — you switch from a condo to a single-family home, for instance — it's worth confirming the number still holds. A quick call to your lender saves a surprise later.

## The bottom line

Pre-approval isn't a box to check before you start touring homes — it's the thing that determines whether you can actually act when you find the right one. Treat it as step one, not a formality.
