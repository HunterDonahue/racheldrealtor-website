This is the question I get asked more than almost any other from sellers: fix it up first, or list it as-is and let the buyer sort it out? The honest answer is that it depends less on the house than most people think, and more on three specific things.

## Start with what's actually broken vs. what's just dated

A cracked driveway or a leaking faucet is a repair. A kitchen with 1990s cabinetry that still functions perfectly is a style preference, not a defect. Buyers price these very differently — repairs feel like red flags, dated finishes just feel like a future project. Know which category you're dealing with before you spend a dollar.

## Cosmetic fixes tend to pay for themselves faster than big ones

A fresh coat of neutral paint, updated light fixtures, and decluttering are inexpensive and consistently help a home show better without much financial risk. A full kitchen remodel is a much bigger bet — you're guessing at what the next buyer will actually want, and there's no guarantee you'll recover what you spend on it at closing.

## Consider your actual timeline

If you need to sell in the next few months, a major renovation probably doesn't make sense — permitting and contractor schedules alone can eat your window. If you have more flexibility, a targeted update to the highest-impact rooms (kitchens and primary bathrooms, generally) is worth evaluating on its own merits, not as a blanket rule.

## The as-is option isn't a last resort

Plenty of buyers are specifically looking for homes with room to put their own stamp on it, especially at a lower price point. Pricing accurately for the home's actual condition and being transparent about it in the listing can work just as well as a renovation — sometimes better, since you're not carrying the risk of a project that runs over budget or past your deadline.
