Buyers looking in 76109 often ask me about Mistletoe Heights and Berkeley Place like they're interchangeable. They're both close to TCU, both known for mature trees and older architecture, and both consistently sought-after — but the actual experience of living in each is different enough that it's worth understanding before you fall in love with one on paper.

## Mistletoe Heights: quieter, more residential

Tucked along the Trinity River, Mistletoe Heights tends to feel more insulated from through-traffic and commercial noise. Lots here are often a bit larger, and the architecture leans toward 1920s-1940s craftsman and Tudor styles. If your priority is a genuine neighborhood feel with less foot traffic, this is usually the one people mean when they describe that.

## Berkeley Place: closer to the action

Berkeley Place sits nearer to the university and the shops and restaurants along the nearby commercial corridors, which means more walkability at the cost of a bit more ambient activity — game days especially. The housing stock is similarly historic but the day-to-day feel is noticeably more connected to what's happening around campus.

## Price doesn't always tell you what you'd expect

Both neighborhoods see strong, consistent demand, and specific lot size, condition, and location within the neighborhood matter more to price than which of the two you're in. Don't assume one is a "step up" from the other — they're different products for different priorities, not tiers of the same thing.

## How to actually choose

If you're deciding between the two, spend time in each at different points in the week — a Tuesday afternoon and a Saturday during a home game will tell you more than any listing photo will. The right one is genuinely about fit, not resale logic.
