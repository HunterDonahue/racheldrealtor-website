Getting a home ready to show doesn't require a full remodel — most of what actually influences a buyer's first impression is achievable in a weekend with the right priorities. Here's where to spend your effort, room by room.

## Entryway: set the tone immediately

Buyers form an impression before they're fully through the door. A clean entry, a working porch light, and a front door that isn't sticking or scuffed does more for a first impression than almost anything else in the house, and it costs almost nothing to fix.

## Kitchen: clear the counters, not the cabinets

You don't need to remodel a dated kitchen to show it well. Clearing counters down to just a couple of intentional items, making sure everything is clean and odor-free, and fixing any obviously broken hardware goes further than people expect. Buyers can picture their own kitchen in a clean, uncluttered space far more easily than a busy one.

## Bathrooms: this is where buyers judge maintenance

Fairly or not, buyers use bathroom condition as a proxy for how well the whole house has been cared for. Recaulking, replacing an old shower curtain, and making sure everything is spotless matters disproportionately here relative to how little it costs to do.

## Bedrooms and closets: show the space, not the stuff

Closets specifically get opened during showings — buyers are checking storage. An overstuffed closet reads as "not enough storage" even if the issue is just belongings. Pack away anything you're not actively using before you list; it does double duty as a head start on your eventual move.

## Outside: don't skip the yard

Mowed grass, trimmed edges, and a clear walkway matter more than most sellers expect, especially for photos — a lot of buyers decide whether to even schedule a showing based on the exterior photo alone.

## The common thread

None of this requires a big budget. It requires seeing your home the way a stranger will see it for the first time, which is genuinely hard to do in a house you've lived in for years.
